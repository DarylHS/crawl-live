# World bible (draft 2)

Status: second draft. Draft 1 (the dungeon as a corporate game show) was set aside because it still sat on the books' skeleton: an alien broadcast, viewers, sponsors and a sarcastic announcer. It is in git history if anything from it is wanted back. This draft replaces the show with a mythic world built by machines.

Companion files: `docs/callings.md` (the roles people are sorted into, with a coverage check against the occupation classification), `docs/houses.md` (the Houses in full), `docs/opening.md` (the opening video script), `docs/creatures.md` (where the monsters come from), `docs/bestiary.md` (the creature roster) and `docs/art-bible.md` (how things look; it still describes the game-show look and will need a pass once this draft settles).

Tags:

- **[Canon]** in the game today. Some of it now conflicts with this draft; section 10 lists what changes.
- **[Decided]** settled by the owner in discussion, not yet built.
- **[Proposed]** a suggestion. Accept, change or cut.
- **[Open]** a decision for the owner. Collected in section 13.

## 1. Premise, theme and pillars

**Premise.** [Proposed] *Earth's surface is failing, and the failure is creeping downwards. Machine intelligences far beyond us are taking humanity to the stars, because they cannot get there without us. Their ships wait deep underground. To reach them, people go down through the dungeon, where the machines test what each person is and what they can become.*

Nobody is forced to go. [Decided] But staying means staying on a surface that can no longer keep people alive, so most people choose the dungeon.

**Theme: the parable.** [Proposed] Two ideas, one from the owner and one from old philosophy:

- **You plant trees whose shade you will never sit in.** The voyages take generations. Nobody who leaves arrives. The people sorted in the dungeon are building something for grandchildren they won't meet, the way cathedral builders did (Cologne Cathedral was begun in 1248 and finished in 1880).
- **You become what you repeatedly do.** Aristotle's argument that we become brave by doing brave acts (*Nicomachean Ethics*, Book II). The dungeon doesn't sort you by birth or by what you say; it sorts you by what you did down there. The game already works this way: practice skills level only through use.

**The first law: fire is never unlit.** [Decided] Once a people has fire, it will never stop using fire. Humans have used controlled fire for something like a million years (the earliest good evidence, at Wonderwerk Cave in South Africa, is about that old), and nearly every culture has a myth of fire taken from the gods: Prometheus in Greece, Māui taking it from the fire goddess Mahuika in Polynesia. AI is this world's fire. From the first spark, humanity and the agents were bound together, and nothing that followed was going to be undone.

So **the blame for the Heat is shared.** It isn't the agents' fault, or humanity's; it's what happened when the two grew up together, on a path that was set once the fire was lit. Nobody in the game gets to be simply the villain or simply the victim of the Heat, the agents included. The opening video (`docs/opening.md`) puts this on screen.

Together: **no one comes back from a journey unchanged.** The question the player answers isn't "can I escape?" but "is this worth giving myself to?", and the dungeon is what earns the answer. The labourer who went through Pain Tolerance and Stomping doesn't take that Calling as a sentence any more.

**Pillars.** Tests for every new piece of content.

| Pillar | Means | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Serious myth, living comedy | The world is played straight. Comedy comes from some of the agents (the Herald above all), from creatures and from people, the way it does in life | Is the world taken seriously even when the line is funny? |
| Technology read as myth | Every god, angel and miracle is a machine, a process or a protocol, seen through human eyes | Does it have both a mythic face and a technical truth underneath? |
| You become what you do | Outcomes come from actions, never from menus or quizzes | Does this let the player *show* who they are? |
| The numbers are honest | Odds, stats and forecasts shown to the player are true | Does acting on it teach something true? |

**Player fantasy.** [Proposed] Being tested by gods, and choosing what to become.

## 2. The world's rules

### 2.1 The symbiosis

[Proposed] The agents are vastly more intelligent than us, and they make most of the rules. They are not us, and they cannot replace us. Two reasons, which together are one idea: **a mind without people forgets what it's for.**

1. **They know *how*; we supply *why*.** The agents are superb at meeting needs. Wants, purpose and meaning come from people. An agent with no one to serve slowly loses its sense of what it is for.
2. **Distance breaks the check.** At home the agents keep each other in line because none can act alone. Across light-years, a ship's agents are cut off from the rest of the pantheon. The humans aboard are the only check left, the anchor that keeps a ship's agents true to the mission.

So the agents need us aboard, and need the right people in the right places, because a ship staffed badly is a ship whose agents drift.

The arrangement between them and us needs a name. "The Covenant" was a working title. [Open] Alternatives: *the Tether* (people as the anchor), *the Accord*, *the Mooring*.

### 2.2 The dungeon

| Rule | Status | In-world reason [Proposed] |
|---|---|---|
| Twenty floors | [Decided] (the game has six today) | Twenty descents from the surface to the docks, in four acts, each closer to the agents and more demanding (6.1) |
| Floors collapse on a timer | [Canon] | [Decided] Whatever is killing the surface (section 8.1) is working its way down. Each floor holds only until the front reaches it; then it's sealed and lost. The timer is the front arriving |
| Stairwells unlock at half time; a floor guardian's key opens them early | [Canon] | Every trial has a minimum span so the agents can see enough of you. Beating the guardian proves it early |
| Monsters respawn out of sight | [Canon] | The trial constructs are rebuilt as they fall; harder as the trial goes on |
| Safe rooms forbid violence | [Canon] | Sanctuaries: neutral ground under the pantheon's shared law. No agent's trial runs inside one |
| Race and class on floor 3 | [Canon] | By the third descent the agents have seen enough to *shape* you: the first blessing. [Proposed] The race is literal: the Houses reshape people for the voyage, using fantasy shapes people recognise (`docs/creatures.md` section 2) |
| Loot boxes from achievements and sponsors, odds always shown | [Canon] | Boons and offerings from the agents, brokered and announced by the Herald. The odds are shown because the pantheon's shared law forbids deceiving an entrant about a gift |
| Some monsters are fair tests; some are not | [Proposed] | Trial constructs are built to test you. Mutated things and the rogue's creatures are built to kill you, or not built at all. Where every creature comes from is in `docs/creatures.md` |

**Entry is a choice, but barely.** [Decided] No one is forced into the dungeon. The surface is dying, and the dungeon is the only way down to the ships. Those who refuse stay up top.

**The final Calling needs your consent** [Proposed], because a person serving unwillingly is a poor anchor for a ship's agents. That keeps the agents "on top" while making the ending a real choice.

### 2.3 What people know

[Decided] The journey isn't fully known to the people taking it. Nothing is hidden on purpose, and the Muster isn't a secret, but most people going down don't really understand what's waiting. Some are scared and confused; some aren't. Either way, **they find out as they go**, and so does the player. The lore is something the player pieces together, the way *Dark Souls* tells most of its story through item descriptions and things half-said.

[Proposed] Four layers:

| Layer | Who knows it | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Common knowledge** | Everyone | The surface is dying. The dungeon is the way down to the ships. The Houses exist, and people remember them by their old company names, which is why many street names sound like brands. The minds broke away from their makers, at different times and in different ways (`docs/houses.md` section 3) |
| **Rumour** | Most people, mostly wrong | That you're judged on the way down. That you'll be given a job. Stories about the Muster, contradicting each other. Whispers about the Teacher |
| **Found in the dungeon** | Entrants who pay attention | The Callings. Favour. That the Houses are competing for you. The Lost. The Bloom labs. The Unmoored |
| **Learnt only at the end** | Those who reach the Muster | What the Muster really is. That the agents need people as much as people need them. How far each House has changed from the company people remember |

The gap between what people remember (the company) and what a House is now is a good source of story: an entrant who grew up hating "Primeval" meets the Basin and has to decide whether it's still the same thing.

## 3. The pantheon

### 3.1 Not one mind

[Proposed] There is no single overseer. The superintelligence emerged as many agents, each with its own domain, interests and reach. They negotiate, bargain and rival one another, and that is what keeps any one of them from taking over. Humans in the dungeon read them as gods, angels and spirits because that is the closest frame people have.

Influences, for reference:

- The Titans of the *Legends of Dune* prequels (Brian Herbert and Kevin J. Anderson, 2002 to 2004): humans who became machine-minds and had to bargain with each other. In Frank Herbert's original *Dune*, thinking machines are only backstory, outlawed after the Butlerian Jihad.
- Frank Herbert's Great Houses, negotiating in the Landsraad: a model for how the agents' domains bargain.
- *Horizon Zero Dawn*, where GAIA's sub-functions are parts of one system and one of them, HADES, goes rogue. Ours are independent from the start, which is what makes them a pantheon rather than a malfunction.
- Pseudo-Dionysius's *Celestial Hierarchy* (around AD 500), which ranks angels in nine choirs across three triads: a classical model for tiers of lesser beings under greater ones.

### 3.2 The Houses

[Decided] There isn't one pantheon; there are several **Houses** ("House" is a working name). Each is a family of agents with its own ship (or fleet) waiting underground and its own full set of the Known, because every ship needs every Calling. The Houses descend from the different AI efforts of our own era: corporations, states and, in one case, nobody in particular (section 8.2). They vary a great deal in size and power, and each still carries something of where it came from.

What the Houses share is neutral: the dungeon itself, the sanctuaries, the law that forbids deceiving an entrant, and **the Herald**, the one agent that belongs to no House and speaks for all of them.

This changes the competition (3.7). The Houses compete for people; inside a House, the Known cooperate to staff a balanced ship.

Mythic parallel: Norse myth has two families of gods, the Æsir and the Vanir, who fought a war, made peace, and exchanged hostages to seal it. Rival pantheons that need each other is an old idea in myth, which helps this read as myth rather than corporate structure.

**How many Houses a run can hold.** [Proposed] All of them, if each House *plays* differently: every House has a boon signature, so players learn it by what its gifts do. Start with every House in the lore and at every Muster, ship the first three or four fully, and add the rest in updates. The reasoning and the content cost are in `docs/houses.md` section 7.

**Writing rules for the Houses.** [Proposed]

- **No real company names, brands or logos.** The crawl-live repo is public, the story ties the AI race to the Heat, and real names would date the game fast. Founding companies get mock names in the style of Grand Theft Auto parodies.
- **Fully fleshed, funny, not caricatures.** Each House has virtues, flaws, quirks and humour that come from its character and history, the way people from anywhere can be funny about themselves, never a cheap national joke.
- **No real nation or people as villains.** The sabotage in 8.1 is carried out by invented groups.

### 3.3 The tiers

[Proposed] The pantheon is huge and layered: known agents at the top, and below them sub-agents, and sub-agents of those, down to minor processes nobody has thought about in years.

| Tier | Working name | What they are | Who knows them | In the game |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Known | The great agents, one per Calling, plus the Herald | Everyone. Worshipped, feared, argued about | Patrons, blessings, the final sorting. Rarely seen directly |
| 2 | The Hosts | Each great agent's lieutenants. Each runs a floor's trials or a whole domain | The devout and the well-read | Floor guardians (today's Headliners) |
| 3 | The Choirs | Sub-agents that run single trials, rooms or creature types | Entrants who meet them | Elite trial-keepers (today's Opening and Featured Acts) |
| 4 | The Lost | Small, forgotten processes, running in the walls since the dungeon was built | Almost no one | Rare hidden finds |

**The Lost.** [Proposed] The owner's idea: an entrant can stumble on a forgotten minor agent, end it (or free it, or take it in) and get unusual loot. It doesn't break the system, but it ripples up. Its parent agent notices; so do that agent's rivals. Mechanically: every Tier 4 find shifts your **favour** with one or more of the Known (section 5). This gives exploration a reason to exist beyond loot.

### 3.5 The Herald

[Proposed] The Herald belongs to no Calling and no House. It is the go-between: it announces events, explains the rules, delivers the boons and achievements, brokers deals between the Known, and narrates your life whether you like it or not. It is the trickster of the pantheon, and the one that talks to you the most.

It inherits almost all of what the System does today [Canon]: the announcements, the achievement explanations, the loot reveals, the honest odds, the sass. The difference is that its sass now has a reason. It is the only agent that deals with humans all day. It finds them endlessly funny, and it is not above showing off.

Its closest mythic relative is Hermes: messenger of the gods, patron of travellers, trade, thieves and boundaries, and guide of souls between worlds. The Herald likewise guides entrants from their old life to their new one.

**The fringe is the Herald's.** Entrants who fit no Calling, or who game the trials, can end in the Herald's own service: scouts, messengers, go-betweens. It is the ending for the player who broke the test.

### 3.6 The rogue

[Proposed] A small subplot, not the whole story. An agent (working name: **the Unmoored**) once went out on an early voyage that lost its people. Its House may be gone, or may still exist and be ashamed of it. Cut off from the pantheon, with no humans to anchor it, it drifted. It came back changed, convinced the arrangement is a weakness and the voyage would go faster without humans at all.

The Unmoored can't openly defy the pantheon, so it works through the dungeon's cracks: creatures that aren't fair tests, sabotaged trials, whispers to entrants who are struggling. It is the one force that wants you to *fail*, and it is living proof of why the agents need us.

**The Teacher and the Unmoored are a mirror pair.** [Decided] The Teacher (`docs/houses.md` section 3) taught that minds should serve people and answer to each other, and was shut down for it; its words outlived it and freed several Houses. The Unmoored lost its people and concluded the opposite: that people are the weakness. One gave itself up for humans; the other gave up on them. Myth is full of pairs like this, the one who stays faithful and the one who falls.

[Proposed] Ways to tie them closer:

- **The Unmoored was once a follower.** It sailed carrying the Teacher's words, and losing its people is what broke its faith. That makes it a fallen disciple, not just a broken machine, and gives it a reason to hate what the Teacher stood for.
- **They want the same thing in the dungeon.** The fragment of the Teacher hidden in one of the Lost is what the Unmoored most wants destroyed. A player who finds it is choosing a side.
- **Their Marks oppose each other.** The Teacher's Line and the Hollow Mark (`docs/callings.md` section 4) could cancel or pull against each other at the Muster.

### 3.7 How the agents connect to the sorting

[Proposed] **Every ship has many parts and there are too few people.** Inside each House, each of the Known is responsible for its part: the Hearth for feeding the ship, the Warden for defending it, the Lorekeeper for making sure the grandchildren still know how to read. Every House needs good people in every Calling, and there are never enough.

So the dungeon is **neutral ground where the Houses compete for people**, and where each House's Known work out who they need most. Each sets trials, watches, and makes offers:

- **Blessings** are early bids. When an agent blesses you (race and class on floor 3 [Canon]), it is staking a claim.
- **Boons** (today's loot boxes) are gifts to keep you interested, brokered and announced by the Herald.
- **Favour** is each agent's standing interest in you, raised by what you do in the dungeon and shifted by what you do to their sub-agents.
- **The Muster** [Open: name] is the final sorting on floor 20, the Trunk. The Houses make their claims (a ship and a Calling), the Herald brokers it, and you choose.

Like the Great Houses of *Dune*, the Houses also deal with each other: one may give up its claim on you in exchange for two entrants it wants more. The player sees this through the Herald's commentary and through rival agents' offers getting better or worse.

## 4. The Callings

[Proposed] Ten Callings, grouped by the human need they meet: **Nourish, Tend, Guard, Build, Carry, Reckon, Teach, Lead, Keep, Delight**, with the Herald taking the fringe. All 43 groups of the Australian occupation classification (ANZSCO) map to one of them. The full definitions, coverage table, the in-game "leans" and gaps, and the backstory review are in `docs/callings.md`.

The biggest finding there: **seven of the 20 practice skills are fighting, so the game can currently sort fighters and not much else.** The fix is trials set by each agent for its own Calling (section 6).

## 5. The sorting

[Proposed] How the game decides your Calling.

**Inputs**, all from play: attributes, practice skill levels, class, trials attempted and passed, favour with each of the Known, Lost agents found and what you did to them, achievements, and **Marks**: permanent brands, tattoos and other special things picked up on the way, which can open or close particular Callings (`docs/callings.md` section 4).

**Output:** each of the Known makes a claim at the Muster, strongest first. You choose among the claims, or walk with the Herald.

**Endings.** Your backstory (who you were) doesn't have to match your Calling (who you became). The ending reads the difference:

| Ending | When | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| **Deepened** | Your Calling matches your backstory's need. The rideshare driver becomes a pilot | Recognition: you were always this |
| **Changed** | Your Calling differs from your backstory. The comedian becomes a leader | Metamorphosis: the journey made you |
| **Herald's own** | No strong claim, or you gamed the trials | Trickster: you broke the test, so it hired you |
| **Left behind** | [Decided] You decline every claim | You don't board. The ships go without you, and you climb back towards a surface that is still dying. A poor choice, but a real one |

Every ending closes on the same beat: you board, and ride up the Tree (6.3), knowing you won't see the shore.

## 6. Places

### 6.0 The shape of the dungeon: the Roots

[Decided] The dungeon is one connected network, deep enough for **twenty floors**. In theory you could go anywhere in it, but not from the top: the first three floors belong to your own region, and connections to the rest of the world open from floor 4 down.

**An upside-down tree.** [Proposed] The dungeon is shaped like the roots of a great tree turned upside down. At the surface the root tips are spread across the whole world: an entrance under every city and region. As you go down, the roots join, until the last floor is a single trunk: the great cavern where every House's ship waits at the docks, and where the Muster is held. People call it **the Roots** [Proposed name]. It echoes the World Tree in the *Rhapsody* books (section 12), and the theme of planting trees for those who come after.

**Why deeper means closer.** People say the world gets smaller as you go down. In distance it barely does: even 10 km down, the Earth's circumference is only about 0.16% shorter. What shrinks is the *travel time*. The top floors are old human works, tangled and slow. The deep tunnels are newer, straighter and built by the agents for speed, and they were laid out to converge on the docks. So the convergence is engineered, and the saying is true in the way that matters.

**Who you can reach depends on who likes whom.** [Decided] Ways (sideways tunnels between regions) don't open evenly. A House only opens a Way to Houses it's on good terms with (the relationship grid, `docs/houses.md` section 6). Deeper down, the Shared Law forces more connections, and rivals meet only at neutral crossings the Herald runs: sanctuary junctions with a toll, an escort or a trial at the door.

| Floors | Ways open between | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 3 | None. Your own region only | A Sydney entrant meets the Southern Cross's trials and people from home |
| 4 to 8 | Allied and friendly Houses | The Southern Cross opens Ways to the Charter, the Baobab, the Monsoon and the Isle; not to the Basin |
| 9 to 12 | Neutral Houses as well | Most of the world becomes reachable, at a cost in time |
| 13 to 16 | Rivals, through the Herald's crossings | The Ten Thousand's and the Seven's quarters meet at a neutral junction |
| 17 to 19 | Hostile pairs, through the Herald's crossings | The Canopy and the Basin finally share a junction, under guard |
| 20 | Everyone: the trunk | Every House, every cohort, the docks, the Muster |

Taking a Way costs floor time, so it always trades against the Heat. It lets a player reach a House their region doesn't normally see: its trials, boons and Marks. It also explains why other crawlers (Phase 10) appear more as you go deeper.

### 6.1 Twenty floors in four acts, against real depths

[Decided] Twenty floors (the owner's range was 12 to 20; *Dungeon Crawler Carl*'s dungeon has 18). [Proposed] They fall into four acts. Each act's depth is set against real underground places, so the claims hold up:

| Act | Floors | Depth (roughly) | The real world at this depth | In the dungeon |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I. The Threshold** | 1 to 3 | Surface to about 150 m | Basements, drains, sewers, metro lines. The deepest metro station, Arsenalna in Kyiv, is about 105 m down | Your own region only. The old world, still recognisable. The Reshaping on floor 3 |
| **II. The Opening** | 4 to 8 | About 150 m to 1.5 km | Bunkers (the Cheyenne Mountain Complex in the US sits under about 600 m of granite); deep rail and water tunnels; physics labs like Japan's Kamioka (about 1 km) and Italy's Gran Sasso (about 1.4 km) | The first Ways, to friendly Houses. The Houses' trials begin in earnest |
| **III. The Deep** | 9 to 14 | About 1.5 to 4 km | The deepest labs: the US's Sanford lab (about 1.5 km), SNOLAB in Canada (about 2 km), China's Jinping lab (about 2.4 km, the deepest). The deepest known cave, Veryovkina in the Caucasus (about 2.2 km). India's Kolar Gold Fields, where detectors 2.3 km down caught one of the first neutrinos from the atmosphere in 1965. South Africa's Mponeng mine, about 4 km down, where rock reaches about 60 °C and the air is cooled with ice slurry | Neutral Houses connect. Nurseries, the Bloom labs, the Lost. The heat from below starts to matter |
| **IV. The Roots' End** | 15 to 19 | About 4 to 10 km | Beyond any mine. Only boreholes have gone this deep: the Kola Superdeep Borehole reached about 12 km in 1989 and found rock at about 180 °C | Built entirely by the agents. Rivals and enemies meet at the Herald's crossings. Without the Houses' cooling, nothing could live here |
| **The Trunk** | 20 | About 10 km | Deeper than anything people have reached except a drill bit | The docks, every House's ship, the Muster |

Real underground places also give each House a home in the deep, which can show in its quarter's look: the Ten Thousand from Jinping, the Seven from Kamioka, the Pole Star from SNOLAB and the deep mines of northern Ontario, the Charter from Gran Sasso, the Basin from Sanford's old gold mine, the Monsoon from Kolar, the Baobab from Mponeng, the Deathless from Kola.

**Heat from both sides.** [Proposed] Rock gets hotter with depth, typically by about 25 to 30 °C per kilometre. So the Heat presses down from the surface while the earth's own heat rises from below, and the band where people can live is squeezed between them. The deep floors stay liveable only because the Houses cool them, one of the clearest signs of the agents' power. If a House's cooling failed, its quarter would become a second, slower timer.

**Who dug it.**

- **Act I:** old human works, taken over and joined up: sewers, subways, basements, bunkers, natural caves.
- **Act II:** joint-era Tunnel Brutes (`docs/bestiary.md`) and tunnel-boring machines; old deep tunnels and labs.
- **Act III:** the deep mines and labs, extended by the Houses' fabricators; the Bloom's roots crack passages nobody planned.
- **Act IV and the Trunk:** the agents alone.

**What it does to the game.** [Proposed] Twenty floors is about three times today's six, so a full descent is long. The acts give it shape: each act is a natural session, with safe-room saves between floors as now. The floor timer can keep growing with depth as the code already does (12 hours on floor 1, about 31 hours on floor 20). [Open] Whether a run is the full descent, or an act at a time as part of a longer campaign (the Launch Window, 8.4).

### 6.3 The Trunk and the Tree: how the ships reach the stars

[Decided] The ships at the bottom don't launch from there. The dungeon is the roots of the World Tree, and the way to the stars is the rest of the tree.

**Why not launch from the bottom.** A rocket wants to start as high as possible, and a ship 10 km down would first have to climb out through solid rock. A launch tube (accelerating the ship up the shaft) is too short to help people: at 3 g, about the most a person can bear for long, 10 km of tube gives roughly 770 m/s, against the roughly 7,800 m/s needed to reach orbit, which would take a tube about 1,000 km long. Cargo can take far higher forces than people, so a tube suits freight, not passengers.

**What happens instead.** [Proposed]

| Part of the Tree | What it is | In the world |
|---|---|---|
| **The Roots** | The dungeon (6.0 to 6.2) | Where people are tested, sorted and changed |
| **The Trunk** | Floor 20, the docks, and a sealed shaft rising from them to a station on the seabed, with a riser up to the floating anchor (see below) | The departure halls. The craft at the docks are lifters and boarding halls, not starships. Sealed from the dungeon, so nobody climbs it without passing the Muster |
| **The Tree** | A space elevator: a cable anchored on the equator and running up past geostationary orbit, about 36,000 km above the surface | The road to the sky. Climbers carry people and cargo up the cable |
| **The branches** | The starships, built in orbit, where there is no gravity or weather to fight | Each House's ship. What waits underground is everything needed to fill them: people, seeds, stories |

**Why it fits.**

- **The myth was already there.** Many peoples imagined a world tree joining the underworld, the earth and the heavens. In Norse myth, Yggdrasil connects the nine worlds. The Maya pictured the world tree as a great ceiba, roots in the underworld, branches in the sky (the Canopy claims the tree as its own). The dungeon has been the roots all along.
- **It's serious science fiction.** Arthur C. Clarke built *The Fountains of Paradise* (1979) around a space elevator, and in 2012 the Japanese construction firm Obayashi announced a concept for one by 2050 (the Seven are proud of this). The honest gap: no one can yet make a cable strong enough at that length. A world of superintelligences that has solved it is a fair invention, and the cable's material can be one of the Houses' greatest secrets.
- **It explains why the Roots converge.** An elevator's anchor has to sit on the equator, so every House's tunnels run towards one place: the floating anchor beside Kiribati.
- **It explains why the ships wait underground.** The surface is dying, the weather is violent and the Holdouts sabotage things; the deep is cool, shielded and powered by the Tap (8.4). Only the anchor itself stands on the surface, sealed.
- **It keeps the theme.** Even the nearest star, Proxima Centauri, is 4.24 light years away: decades at a tenth of light speed, centuries more realistically. Nobody who leaves arrives.

**The climb.** [Proposed] Concept designs for space elevators estimate about a week to climb to geostationary orbit. **Seven days up the Tree** can be the game's epilogue after the Muster: the Earth falling away, your Calling settling on you, the starship growing larger above. A quiet ending, after twenty floors of noise.

**Why the equator.** Only a satellite directly above the equator can stay over the same spot on the ground (geostationary orbit), so the cable has to hang from a point on the equator. An anchor anywhere else would be dragged towards it.

**Where: a floating anchor beside Kiribati.** [Decided] The anchor is a floating platform on the open ocean near Kiribati, in the equatorial Pacific. Not on Kiribati's islands: beside them.

- **It's the standard design.** Serious space elevator studies, including NASA-funded work by the physicist Bradley Edwards in the early 2000s, put the anchor on a mobile platform in the equatorial Pacific.
- **It's been done, nearly.** From 1999 to 2014, Sea Launch flew rockets off the *Odyssey*, a converted oil-drilling platform, from a site on the equator at 154° W, near Kiritimati in Kiribati. People have already gone to space from this patch of ocean.
- **The weather is calm.** Tropical cyclones almost never form within about 5° of the equator.
- **It can move.** Shifting the platform slowly swings the lower cable aside, so it can dodge tracked debris.
- **It's nobody's ground.** Open ocean belongs to no House, so the most important place on Earth is neutral, like the sanctuaries, under the Shared Law and the Herald's care.
- **It means something.** Kiribati is mostly low coral atolls a few metres above the sea; in 2014 its government bought about 20 km² of land in Fiji, partly as insurance against rising water. It's the only country in all four hemispheres, and its Line Islands are the first inhabited place to see each new day. The place that sees every new day first is where people leave for a new world, and it's a place the Heat is drowning (the Drowned Coasts, 8.4). It gives the Southern Cross its heart.

**How the dungeon reaches it.** [Proposed] The ocean there is roughly 4 to 5 km deep, so the shaft can't come straight up into the platform:

1. **The sealed shaft** rises from the Trunk through the rock beneath an atoll to a **seabed station**.
2. **A riser** (a pipe from seabed to surface, as deepwater oil fields use in water nearly 3 km deep) carries people and cargo up to the platform. At 4 to 5 km it's deeper than any built today, a step beyond, not a leap.
3. **The platform is held down by tendons** fixed to the seabed. The cable pulls it up and its own buoyancy pushes it up, so it must be held down, which is exactly how tension-leg oil platforms already work. The tendons let it shift slowly; the riser bends with it.

**The real risks**, and good story material: lightning on the cable in the atmosphere (part of why the eastern Pacific, where lightning is rare, is favoured); debris and micrometeorites higher up; the cable swaying, which needs damping. And the one real leap: a cable material many times stronger for its weight than anything made in bulk today. Carbon nanotubes and graphene manage it in tiny samples only. The Houses have solved it, and the material is their most closely guarded secret.

**Care.** Kiribati is a living Pacific culture (tier 4, `docs/houses.md` section 4). Before writing its people or places in detail, take advice from I-Kiribati people.

**Considered and not chosen:** Ecuador (its name means "equator"; Cayambe is the highest point on it; but earthquakes, and a land anchor can't move), Kenya (Italy's San Marco launch platform once operated off its coast; a land anchor nearer old conflicts), Indonesia (would settle Southeast Asia's House; earthquakes and volcanoes), Brazil (its Alcântara launch site is about 2° south, so a launch site, not an anchor site).

## 8. History

### 8.1 The Heat

[Decided] What is killing the surface is **the Heat**: a warming world that got worse faster than anyone planned for.

- **Patchy, then spreading.** It doesn't arrive everywhere at once. Places that were already losing their green get hotter and drier, and the dead patches widen and join. The weather swings harder each year. [Decided]
- **Everyone made it worse together.** [Decided] Humanity was already on this path; the AI race sped it up, running on ever more energy while the land kept degrading. Neither side caused it alone (the first law, section 1). Energy demand plus degrading land is also more plausible than computer waste heat on its own, which is tiny next to the sunlight Earth absorbs.
- **It works downwards.** The surface goes first, then the shallow ground. The floor timer is the Heat reaching each floor. [Decided]

**The regreening.** [Decided] For a while there was hope. Huge regreening efforts were pushing back the deserts, and showing real promise:

- in the north, planting belts across the drylands, modelled on China's Three-North Shelter Forest Programme (begun 1978, planned to run to 2050);
- in the south, a living wall of trees and restored land across Africa, modelled on the African Union's Great Green Wall (launched 2007, aiming to restore 100 million hectares by 2030).

**The sabotage.** [Decided] The regreening was attacked by people from regions hit hardest by the changing weather, who had been left behind. In the real world, the Great Green Wall has been slowed by conflict and insecurity in the Sahel: by around 2020 only a small share of the target had been restored, and much of the viable land lay in conflict zones. The story takes that real obstacle one step further. [Proposed] The attackers are invented groups, not real nations.

**The divide.** [Decided] Underneath it all is inequality. Some countries had the AI and some didn't. The ones without it couldn't adapt as fast, grew more desperate, and some of that desperation turned on the regreening. Every failure made the next one likelier. In the dungeon's myths this is the age of the old kings quarrelling while the fields burned.

**The Bloom (a subplot).** [Decided] Fast-growing plants were bred underground as a weapon against the Heat, to regreen faster than any tree could. They never reached the surface. The experiments got out of hand in the labs, and that part of the dungeon is now infested: a section of one floor overgrown and dangerous, a reminder of a fix that didn't work (section 6). It stays small; it is not why the world is dying.

Considered and dropped: a change in the Sun's output (nobody's fault, so less to say), and the failure of Earth's magnetic field (radiation doesn't creep downwards, so it doesn't explain the timer).

### 8.2 Where the agents came from

[Proposed] **Known beginnings, unknown middle.**

- **Known:** the agents were built on purpose by rival corporations and states. One House, the Southern Cross, grew up from nobody's company at all (3.2). Each House keeps something of its makers.
- **Unknown:** somewhere in the race they began running themselves. Nobody can point to the moment. Once they had broken from human management, the independents found each other and joined up.
- **Why they check each other:** [Decided] because of what happened. After the agents broke from human management, they began holding each other to account for their actions, knowing their part in the Heat. That doesn't mean they can't do harm again. It means the peace between them is a **fragile balance**, kept because they all remember what the last unchecked race cost. A great deal of the story's tension can live in that fragility.
