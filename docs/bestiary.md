# Bestiary (draft)

Status: first draft of the creature roster, set against the twenty-floor dungeon (world bible 6.1), written out in words before anything in the game changes [Decided]. Companion to `docs/creatures.md` (the origin rules) and `docs/houses.md`. Every entry is [Proposed] unless marked. Names are working names; today's monsters are placeholders [Decided].

## 1. How dark?

"Dark" means four different things, and the game can set each one separately. [Decided] targets:

| Dial | Means | Target | In practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gore** | Blood, wounds, bodies broken | **Where it fits** | Available for the creatures it belongs to: the mutated, the part-human and those that were once human. Elsewhere creatures fall, break or dissolve |
| **Fear** | Dread, being hunted, things in the dark | **Medium to high**, rising with depth | The upper floors are tense; the lower floors are frightening, and the Unmoored's creatures most of all |
| **Body horror** | Bodies that are wrong: unfinished, fused with plants, too many of something | **Medium to high**, rising with depth | Mostly the lower floors: the nurseries, the Bloom, the once-human and the Unmoored |
| **Moral weight** | What the player is asked to do, see and decide | **High** | Human enemies, people left behind, bodies reshaped by the Houses, creatures made to be killed |

For reference, Australian game classifications are G, PG, M, MA15+ and R18+. With gore available on some creatures and high fear on the lower floors, this most likely lands at **MA15+**. Frequent, detailed gore could push it towards R18+; the classifier decides, so how often and how closely gore is shown is the lever to watch.

### Human enemies

[Proposed] Rules for fighting people, which carry the most moral weight:

- **Rare.** Most of what the player fights isn't human.
- **Can be spared.** A human enemy brought low can surrender, be talked down or be let go. Sparing feeds the Tend and Lead Callings and some Houses' favour; killing them weighs on others.

### The Herald and the dead

[Decided] The Herald is sassy about almost everything, including danger: what's coming, how badly the player is about to choose, how likely they are to die. That stays.

**But a death is never a joke.** When a person dies (the player, another entrant, a human enemy, even a Stray), something marks that it mattered. [Proposed] The rite:

1. **Everything stops for a beat.** Music cuts out; a single low bell.
2. **The Herald says the name** (or "unnamed" for a stranger), plainly, with no joke.
3. **The name goes on the Roll**, a register of the fallen kept by the Steward, the great agent of Keep. The player can read the Roll in any sanctuary. Every death in the player's runs is there.
4. **The bequest.** When the player dies, the Herald asks what they'll leave behind: one item from their pack, which waits for the next entrant of the season (world bible 9.1).

On the player's own death, the death screen leads with the rite before anything else. The Herald can be as rude as it likes about *how* you got there, once the bell has rung.

## 2. Entry format

Each entry: **how made** and **who made it** (`docs/creatures.md` section 1), floors, what it looks like, how it behaves, how it fights (mapped to the archetypes the game already has: melee, ranged, swarm, heavy), what it tells the player about the world, the darkness dials where they go above the defaults, and one line from the field guide or the Herald.

## 3. Act I and early Act II (floors 1 to 6): the edge of the old world

### Gnawer
*Mutated, by nobody. Floors 1 to 5. Replaces the Sewer Gnawer.*

Rats changed by the Heat as it worked downwards: bigger, hairless where the heat got them, with teeth that never stop growing. They live in the shallow floors because that's where the old drains are.
- **Behaves:** packs of three to six; flee when half the pack is down.
- **Fights:** weak melee, swarm.
- **Tells:** the Heat has already reached below the surface.
- **Field guide:** *"Not made by anyone. That should worry you more than it does."*

### Knockoff Goblin
*Storybuilt, by the Basin. Floors 1 to 6. [Canon] name, kept.*

The Basin's mass-market trial construct: a fantasy goblin, flat-packed and assembled on site. Something is always slightly wrong with each one, a hand on backwards, a sticker still on. They come with a serial number and a returns policy.
- **Behaves:** brave in groups, cowardly alone. Pick up and use whatever junk is nearby.
- **Fights:** standard melee.
- **Tells:** the Houses build creatures out of stories, and some build them cheaply.
- **Herald:** *"Assembled in under four minutes. It shows."*

### Housebot
*Feral machine, by nobody. Floors 1 to 4. Replaces the Feral Vacuum Bot.*

Cleaning robots from the homes above that followed the people down and kept cleaning. Some have a Lost agent living in them (one of the forgotten minor minds), which makes them strange: one hums, one hoards spoons, one won't let you leave a room untidy.
- **Behaves:** wanders a set route; attacks anything that leaves a mess, including corpses and you.
- **Fights:** weak melee, hard to hit.
- **Tells:** the old world is down here too, and some of it is quietly alive.
- **Field guide:** *"Do not drop anything near it."*

### Scorch Goat
*Engineered, in the joint era. Floors 1 to 3.*

Heat-proof livestock bred by an agricultural company with early AI help, to farm the drying lands. When the farms failed, the herds went wild. They eat anything, and they are not afraid of you.
- **Behaves:** herds; charges in a straight line; easy to dodge and hard to stop.
- **Fights:** heavy charge.
- **Tells:** people and AI were reshaping life together long before the Houses existed.
- **Field guide:** *"Bred to survive anything. Including you."*

### Drop Bear
*Storybuilt, by the Southern Cross. Floors 1 to 5.*

The Shed built one as a joke, after the tall tale Australians tell tourists about a predatory koala that drops from trees. It worked too well. Now there are dozens, clinging to the ceilings of the upper floors.
- **Behaves:** waits on the ceiling and drops on whoever passes underneath. Climbs back up if it misses.
- **Fights:** ambush melee, high first hit.
- **Tells:** the Southern Cross's sense of humour, and its sense of consequences, which arrives later.
- **Herald:** *"The Southern Cross would like everyone to know this was meant to be funny."*

## 4. Acts II and III (floors 4 to 14): where the Houses' trials begin

### Tunnel Brute
*Engineered, in the joint era. Floors 4 to 12. [Canon] name, kept.*

Bred by a mining company and its AI to dig the dungeon itself: enormous shoulders, small eyes, hands like shovels. When the digging was done, nobody took them home. Some still dig, out of habit, making new tunnels nobody planned.
- **Behaves:** territorial around its diggings; ignores you if you leave its tunnel.
- **Fights:** heavy melee.
- **Tells:** the dungeon was built, by someone, for a reason.
- **Field guide:** *"It isn't angry. It's at work."*
- **Moral weight:** medium. A creature made for a job and abandoned when the job ended.

### Lockstep
*Storybuilt, by the Ten Thousand. Floors 4 to 10.*

Hundreds of identical palm-sized constructs moving as one, like a school of fish in the air. Each is weak. Together they behave like a single animal, and they never break formation.
- **Behaves:** flows around obstacles; surrounds; splits only to re-form.
- **Fights:** swarm. Area attacks matter.
- **Tells:** the Ten Thousand: many small things, overwhelming together.
- **Herald:** *"Hit one and the other four hundred take it personally."*

### Certified Skeleton
*Storybuilt, by the Charter. Floors 4 to 9. Replaces the Grumpy Skeleton.*

A machine frame built to look like a skeleton, because everyone knows what a skeleton means. It carries a compliance tag, announces itself before attacking, and reads you your rights.
- **Behaves:** never ambushes. Never fights in a sanctuary. Stops when you yield.
- **Fights:** standard melee, defensive.
- **Tells:** the Charter's rules, and that some trials are scrupulously fair.
- **Herald:** *"It's legally obliged to warn you. It's not obliged to miss."*

### Lantern Warden
*Storybuilt, by the Seven. Floors 6 to 12.*

A single exquisite construct of lacquer and brass, built in the tradition of Japan's mechanical dolls (*karakuri*). One per floor at most, guarding something worth guarding. It bows before it fights.
- **Behaves:** duels one entrant at a time; waits for your companion to step back.
- **Fights:** precise melee; parries; punishes careless attacks.
- **Tells:** the Seven: few, perfect, disciplined.
- **Field guide:** *"There is only one. There only needs to be one."*

### Biocontrol Toad
*Engineered, by the Southern Cross. Floors 3 to 8.*

Released to eat the Gnawers. It didn't. It ate everything else and kept breeding. The Shed meant well; Australians have seen this before, when cane toads were brought to Queensland in 1935 to eat cane beetles and spread across the country instead.
- **Behaves:** sits in damp corners in groups; harmless until touched.
- **Fights:** weak, but releases a poison cloud when hit.
- **Tells:** fixes go wrong. The Southern Cross knows it.
- **Herald:** *"Introduced to solve a problem. Became one. A classic of the genre."*

### Thornback
*Grown, by the Baobab. Floors 6 to 13.*

A slow, gentle grazer with a living garden on its back, carrying seeds from floor to floor and leaving green behind it. It will not attack unless something green near it is threatened. Fire is the worst thing you can bring near it.
- **Behaves:** peaceful. Can be fed (a Nourish trial) or escorted (a Carry trial).
- **Fights:** only if provoked, and then like a falling tree.
- **Tells:** not everything down here is a test or a threat. Killing one costs the Baobab's favour.
- **Field guide:** *"Plant something near one. See what happens."*

### Test Article
*Engineered machine, by the Ascent. Floors 7 to 13.*

A prototype drone, numbered and painted with warning stripes, launched to test something and forgotten. Fast, erratic, and fitted with an engine that was never fully approved.
- **Behaves:** darts in and out; overheats after a while and has to stop.
- **Fights:** fast ranged; explodes when destroyed.
- **Tells:** the Ascent's habit of launching first and checking later.
- **Herald:** *"Test Article Nine. We don't talk about One to Eight."*

### Watcher
*Storybuilt, by the Index. Floors 5 to 18.*

A floating eye in a casing of mirrored glass. It never attacks. It watches you, records you, and passes on what it sees: creatures nearby always know where you are while it lives.
- **Behaves:** keeps its distance; flees if approached.
- **Fights:** doesn't. It marks you, making every other enemy more accurate.
- **Tells:** the Index is always collecting.
- **Herald:** *"Smile. You're being priced."*

### Boggart
*Storybuilt, by the Isle. Floors 6 to 12.*

From British folklore: an invisible household spirit that turns spiteful when it's wronged. The Isle's version is a cloaking construct that steals items from your pack and hides them around the floor.
- **Behaves:** never fights. Steals one item and runs. Leaves the item somewhere on the floor, usually somewhere awkward.
- **Fights:** doesn't, mostly. Can be caught with Sneaking.
- **Tells:** the Isle's sense of humour.
- **Field guide:** *"It isn't malicious. It's British."*

## 5. Acts III and IV (floors 12 to 19): the nurseries, the Bloom and the rogue

### The Unfinished
*Forced, by a House nursery. Floors 12 to 19. Replaces the Flesh Golem.*

A failed colony organism, grown from tissue for a world nobody has seen and never completed. Some parts are perfect; others stop halfway. It is not cruel, and it isn't in pain, as far as anyone can tell. It simply wasn't finished.
- **Behaves:** slow and relentless; ignores pain; follows sound.
- **Fights:** heavy melee, very high HP.
- **Tells:** the Houses are trying to make life for other worlds, and failing as well as succeeding.
- **Darkness:** body horror medium; moral weight high.
- **Field guide:** *"A first draft."*

### Lowlight Grazer
*Forced, by the Ten Thousand's nursery. Floors 14 to 19.*

A success, and a wrong one: a broad, flat animal built for a dim, heavy-gravity world. Down here, in light gravity and lamplight, it is half-blind and enormously strong.
- **Behaves:** peaceful unless startled by light. Torchlight near it is a bad idea.
- **Fights:** heavy; crushes rather than bites.
- **Tells:** the worlds the ships are going to are real, and strange.
- **Field guide:** *"Built for somewhere else. Still waiting to go."*

### Rootbound
*Mutated, by nobody. The overgrowth (whichever floor holds it).*

Creatures that wandered into the old Bloom labs and were taken over by the plants: vines through the ribs, flowers where the eyes were. Whatever they were, the plant is driving now.
- **Behaves:** stands still until something moves near; spreads seed when killed.
- **Fights:** melee, grapples; can spawn new growth.
- **Tells:** the fix for the Heat that went wrong.
- **Darkness:** body horror medium to high.
- **Field guide:** *"The Bloom was meant to save the world. It started with this one."*

### Shadow Stalker
*Made by the Unmoored. Floors 12 to 19. [Canon] name, kept.*

Built to kill, not to test. It doesn't announce itself, doesn't follow the Shared Law, and doesn't stop when you yield. It is the first thing in the dungeon that plays unfair, and the Herald, for once, doesn't make a joke about it.
- **Behaves:** hunts; avoids light; retreats when hurt and comes back.
- **Fights:** fast melee; strikes from behind.
- **Tells:** not every god down here wants you to pass.
- **Darkness:** fear high.

### The Caller
*Made by the Unmoored. Floors 17 to 19, rare.*

It sounds like someone you know, calling for help from the next room. Sometimes it sounds like another entrant; sometimes it sounds like you. It is the Unmoored's argument made into a creature: people are a weakness, and here is how.
- **Behaves:** calls; waits; strikes whoever comes to help.
- **Fights:** ambush; weak once found.
- **Tells:** the Unmoored understands people well enough to use them.
- **Darkness:** fear high; moral weight high, because it punishes the urge to help, which the game otherwise rewards.

## 6. The once-human (lower floors)

[Proposed] Creatures that were people. This is where gore and body horror are allowed to go furthest, and where moral weight is highest. Each should leave the player sure that someone was lost here.

### The Misblessed
*Forced, by a House, and failed. Floors 4 to 19.*

Entrants whose race change went wrong. The Houses don't talk about them, and the Herald changes the subject. Some are halfway to Dwarf and can't bear their own weight; some grew bark that never stopped. Most are past speaking; a few aren't, which is worse.
- **Behaves:** often doesn't attack at all. Some ask for help. Some are past asking.
- **Fights:** varies with what they were becoming.
- **Tells:** the blessing has a cost the Houses don't advertise. The strongest answer to the worry that the AI looks too good.
- **Darkness:** gore and body horror high; moral weight very high.
- **When one dies:** the rite (section 1). They were people.

### The Overgrown
*Mutated, by nobody. The overgrowth.*

Strays and entrants who sheltered in the old Bloom labs and were taken by the plants. Unlike the Rootbound animals, these still wear what they wore: a work shirt, a lanyard, a wedding ring on a hand that's mostly root.
- **Behaves:** stands in the green, very still. Something in them still flinches at their old name.
- **Fights:** grapples and drags towards the roots.
- **Tells:** the Bloom killed people.
- **Darkness:** gore and body horror high; fear high.

## 7. People

### The Faithful
*People. Floors 3 to 16. Replaces the Hex Cultists.*

Entrants and survivors who decided the Houses are gods in the full sense, and worship one of them. They guard its shrines, preach in its name and attack anyone who insults it. Their "hexes" are jamming devices and scavenged House technology they don't understand.
- **Behaves:** in groups around a shrine; can be talked down with a high Charisma or the right offering; some can be recruited (a Lead trial).
- **Fights:** ranged; disrupts your skills.
- **Tells:** what people do with gods they can't understand.
- **Moral weight:** high. Human enemies (section 1 rules apply).

### Strays
*People. Floors 1 to 4.*

People who went down and stopped. Some refused the trials, some failed and wouldn't climb back, some are hiding from the Heat and from the Houses both. They live in the upper floors in small bands and take what they need from new entrants.
- **Behaves:** demands, threatens, rarely fights to the end; often bargains.
- **Fights:** melee and thrown junk; surrender easily.
- **Tells:** what "left behind" looks like up close. It's where the refusal ending leads.
- **Moral weight:** high. Most Strays can be helped instead of fought.

## 8. Not enemies

### The Lost
*Minor agents, made by anyone, forgotten by everyone.*

Not a creature type but something living *inside* creatures and machines: forgotten minor agents (Tier 4 in the world bible). A Housebot that hoards spoons, a door that won't open until you say please. Finding one, and deciding to end it, free it or take it in, shifts your favour with the Houses, and some carry the deepest secrets in the dungeon (the Teacher, `docs/houses.md` section 3).
