## The process

Mainly the Snowflake Method (Randy Ingermanson): grow the story outward from its core, one level at a time, with a writers'-room loop for each open question.

| Step | What | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Lock the spine | One sentence and one page | Done: `synopsis.md`. Final check pending |
| 2. Character sheets | Want, need, change, wound, secret and one line for the core cast | Drafted: `characters.md` |
| 3. Open questions | Work through `open-questions.md` one at a time | Next. Starting with the people left behind |
| 4. Longer synopsis | About four pages: one full season, every path and ending | Not started |
| 5. Branch map | Every choice and ending as a flowchart | Started: the Journey chalkboard in the atlas |
| 6. Read-through test | Tell the story to two or three people; fix what confused them | Not started |
| 7. Story lock, version 1.0 | Then assess what goes into the game, and how | Not started |

**The loop for each question** (step 3):

1. **Ask** the question plainly.
2. **Lay out options**, with what each would mean.
3. **Pick one.**
4. **Check the ripples:** what else in the world does this change? Update those places too.
5. **Record it** as [Decided] where it belongs, and tick it off in `open-questions.md`.

Step 4 is the one people skip, and it's what keeps a large world consistent.

## When the story is done

The story is locked when:

1. Every question in `open-questions.md` is answered or deliberately left as mystery.
2. The friend synopsis reads start to finish with no gaps, at a page or two.
3. It passes the stress test again (`world-bible.md` section 16): one breath, theme, cause and effect, rules hold, the player matters.
4. Two or three people have heard it, and what confused them is fixed.

Then: assess what goes into the game, starting from `docs/game/design-notes.md`.
