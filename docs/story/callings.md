# The Callings (draft)

Status: working draft, companion to `docs/story/world-bible.md`. How the game reads a Calling from play (skills, trials, gaps) and the backstory review are parked in `docs/game/design-notes.md` sections 7 and 8. The Callings are the roles the pantheon sorts people into for the voyage. They group work by the **human need it meets**, not by industry or skill level: a chef, a barista and a farmer all *nourish*.

Tags as in the world bible: **[Canon]** in the game, **[Proposed]** suggestion, **[Open]** owner decision.

## 1. The ten Callings

[Proposed] Working names. The patron agents' titles are placeholders until the pantheon is named.

| Calling | Need it meets | Common roles today | Patron (working title) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nourish** | Food and drink | Chef, cook, baker, butcher, barista, bartender, mixologist, waiter, farmer, fisher, gardener, food factory worker | The Hearth |
| **Tend** | Health and care | Doctor, nurse, paramedic, aged-care worker, childcare worker, disability support, counsellor, social worker, vet, parent | The Mender |
| **Guard** | Safety | Soldier, police, firefighter, security guard, lifeguard, emergency services | The Warden |
| **Build** | Shelter, tools, infrastructure | Builder, carpenter, electrician, plumber, mechanic, welder, miner, machine operator, factory worker, engineer-tradie | The Wright |
| **Carry** | Movement and supply | Pilot, driver, sailor, courier, warehouse worker, logistics planner, buyer, sales assistant, checkout operator, trader | The Wayfarer |
| **Reckon** | Understanding | Scientist, engineer, analyst, programmer, accountant, researcher, technician, planner | The Reckoner |
| **Teach** | Passing knowledge on | Teacher, lecturer, trainer, coach, librarian, mentor, teacher's aide | The Lorekeeper |
| **Lead** | Coordination | Executive, manager, supervisor, politician, diplomat, project manager, HR | The Crown |
| **Keep** | Order and record | Lawyer, judge, clerk, administrator, receptionist, bookkeeper, archivist, cleaner, maintenance | The Steward |
| **Delight** | Meaning and morale | Artist, musician, actor, writer, comedian, athlete, game maker, hairdresser, tour guide | The Muse |
| *(none)* | The fringe | Whoever fits no Calling, or games the trials | The Herald (section 4) |

**Why Teach is its own Calling.** [Proposed] On a generation ship, nobody who leaves arrives. Everything the first generation knows has to survive in the next. Teaching is not a sub-type of Reckon on this voyage; it is how the mission survives at all.

**Why Keep exists.** [Proposed] The work that holds a society together without anyone noticing: records, rules, accounts, cleaning. On a ship that runs for generations, it is the difference between a colony and a wreck.

## 2. Coverage check

The test that the Callings cover everything people do: every group in the official occupation classification must map to one.

**Source and caveat.** This check uses the 43 sub-major groups of ANZSCO (Australian and New Zealand Standard Classification of Occupations, v1.3, 2022), which classify every occupation in Australia. The ABS replaced ANZSCO with OSCA (Occupation Standard Classification for Australia) in December 2024. OSCA has 53 sub-major groups, mainly from splitting and reorganising existing ones. Its pages couldn't be reached from this session, so ANZSCO is used here. Because both cover all occupations, a mapping that covers every ANZSCO group should also cover OSCA. Re-check against OSCA when convenient.

Two things fall out of this:

- **The voyage is mostly a building and logistics problem.** Build and Carry hold more groups than anything else, which is true of real economies as well.
- **Guard and Teach are single groups, but large and essential ones.** On the ship they should feel rare and valued rather than minor.

## 3. Marks

[Decided] The Calling is settled at the Muster, at the end of the run. It's shaped by everything the player has become: stats, skills, trials, favour, and special things they came across along the way. Some of those special things are **Marks**: permanent signs on the body, earned or inflicted in the dungeon, that open a Calling, close one, or make the player better at one.

The idea echoes the opening video's Cherokee fire story, where everyone who carried fire came back marked.

[Proposed] Examples:

| Mark | Gained by | Effect at the Muster |
|---|---|---|
| **Kiln Brand** | Passing the Seven's hardest Build trial | Opens the Seven's Wright to you, which they almost never allow. Strong Build claim |
| **Bark Ring** | Sheltering a Thornback through a whole floor | Strong Nourish and Tend claims from the Baobab |
| **Star Tattoo** | Finding all three stairwells on a floor before they open | Strong Carry claim from the Southern Cross: a navigator |
| **Debt Stamp** | Taking an Index loan and not paying it back | The Index claims you first, whatever else you are. Other Houses bid lower |
| **Charter Seal** | Never breaking the Shared Law in a whole run | Every House trusts you more. Closes nothing |
| **Hollow Mark** | Being struck by the Unmoored's creatures and surviving | Closes Guard (the Houses won't arm someone the rogue has touched). But the Herald takes an interest |
| **Teacher's Line** | Finding the fragment of the Teacher in one of the Lost | Opens a hidden Calling path. [Open] To what? |

Marks are permanent, like race changes. A run can collect several, and they can pull against each other, which is where the Muster gets interesting.

[Open] Whether a Human (no race change) is barred from any Calling is still under review (`docs/story/creatures.md` section 2).

## 4. Open questions

1. Are ten Callings right? Merge Keep into Lead, or split Build into Build (infrastructure) and Make (manufacturing)?
2. The patron agents' working titles (the Hearth, the Mender and so on) are placeholders. Mythic names, technical names, or both (a public name and a system designation)?
3. Which trials to build first? Nourish and Reckon are the cheapest, because they can reuse crafting and the existing odds systems.
