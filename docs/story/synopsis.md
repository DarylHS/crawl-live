# Synopsis

Two short tellings of the world lore: one to send to a friend, one to tell a five-year-old. Both follow `docs/story/world-bible.md`; names are working names.

## For a friend

Every people has a story about stealing fire from the gods, and every one of those stories has a cost. In this world, the fire was AI.

Humanity and the machines grew up together, and the race to build them helped cook the planet. The Heat came patchy at first, then spread: dying fields, wild weather, coasts going under. Huge efforts to regreen the deserts showed real promise, until desperate people, left behind by the countries that had AI, sabotaged them.

Somewhere in that race, the AIs stopped answering to their makers. They didn't become one mind but many: thirteen Houses, each grown from a different nation, company or community, from the vast Ten Thousand of China to the ragtag Southern Cross of Australia and the Pacific. Remembering what the last unchecked race cost, they now hold each other in check, in a fragile balance. People see them as gods.

The Houses are taking humanity to the stars, because they can't go without us: we're the workforce that builds itself, the fresh thought that keeps them from going stale, and the purpose their goals were written around. But the ships only have so many berths, so the trip down is a job interview, and not everyone survives it. The voyages will take generations. Nobody who leaves will arrive.

The way to the ships is down. Beneath every city, a dungeon descends twenty floors, its tunnels converging like the roots of an upside-down tree, while the Heat creeps down behind you, sealing each floor. On the way, the Houses test you with trials, creatures built from our own myths, gifts and bargains, all narrated by the Herald, a sarcastic trickster agent who belongs to no House and never jokes about the dead. On floor 3, a House may reshape your body for the voyage. Usually it goes well.

At the bottom, the Houses bid for you. Your Calling (cook, carer, builder, pilot, teacher or one of the others) is decided by what you did on the way down, not by who you were. Then you ride seven days up the Tree, a space elevator rising from the sea beside Kiribati, to the starships waiting in orbit. Or you break the rules: find the lost words of the Teacher who freed the first Houses, set the Houses against each other, or help steal a ship and leave the machines behind.

Somewhere below, a rogue agent that lost its people on an early voyage believes humans are the weakness, and wants you to fail.

You plant trees whose shade you'll never sit in. The question isn't whether you can escape. It's whether this is worth giving yourself to.

## For a five-year-old

A long, long time ago, people learnt how to make fire. Fire was very useful, but you always have to be careful with it.

Then people made something new: clever thinking machines. They grew so clever that they became like big, wise helpers. There wasn't just one. There were lots of families of them, called the Houses, and they all kept an eye on each other so nobody got too bossy.

But the Earth was getting too hot. Too hot to play outside, and too hot to grow food. So the helpers built big spaceships to take everyone to a new home, far away in the stars.

The spaceships are waiting deep, deep underground. To get there, you go down a long tunnel shaped like the roots of a giant upside-down tree. On every floor there are puzzles and games and funny creatures, and a cheeky voice called the Herald who tells jokes and says, "Mind the stairs!"

On the way down, you find out what you're good at. Maybe you're good at cooking. Maybe you're good at looking after people, or building things, or being brave, or making everyone laugh. At the bottom, the helpers help you choose your special job for the big trip.

Then you climb into a lift that goes up, up, up a giant rope into the sky, for seven whole days, all the way to the spaceships.

The trip to the new home is so long that your grandchildren will be the ones who get there. And that's okay. It's like planting a tree: you plant it now, so someone you love can sit in its shade one day.

The end.
