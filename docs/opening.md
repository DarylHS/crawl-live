# Opening sequence (draft)

Status: first draft of a short opening video, about 75 to 90 seconds. Companion to `docs/world-bible.md`; it puts the bible's first law (fire is never unlit) on screen. Tags as in the bible.

## Purpose

Set the tone before the first floor: **serious myth, living comedy.** The narration is played straight from start to finish. The Herald gets one line at the very end, so the player meets the game's voice of comedy only once the myth has landed.

The structure:

1. Every people has a story of fire taken from the gods.
2. Every one of those stories has a cost.
3. Our fire was a mind. The cost is here now.
4. The blame is shared. Nobody gets to be the villain.
5. Humanity is struggling; the Houses keep a fragile balance.
6. The way down is open. You won't come back the same.

## Script

Narrator: an older voice, unhurried. [Open] Possibly the Lorekeeper, the great agent of Teaching. A human narrator works as well.

## Notes

**The myths.** All three are real, and each has a cost, which is why they were chosen:

- **Prometheus** (Greek, told by Hesiod): punished by Zeus, chained to a rock where an eagle ate his liver each day; humanity was punished with Pandora.
- **Māui and Mahuika** (Polynesian, told across many island groups; the Māori version is best known in Australia and New Zealand): Māui talks Mahuika out of her fingernails of fire one at a time; she sends fire after him and it nearly burns everything, until rain puts it out and the last sparks hide in certain trees, which is why rubbing their wood together makes fire.
- **The First Fire** (Cherokee, recorded by James Mooney in *Myths of the Cherokee*, 1900): the animals each try to fetch fire from a burning sycamore and come back scorched. Raven is burned black, and the owls' eyes are marked. Only the Water Spider succeeds, carrying a coal in a bowl woven on her back.

The Cherokee story fits the game especially well: **carrying fire changes the one who carries it.**

**Respect.** The Māori and Cherokee stories are living traditions, not public-domain props. [Proposed] Retell them faithfully, credit them in the game, and for a commercial release ask advisers from those communities to check the retellings. Aboriginal Australian fire stories exist too, but they belong to particular nations and their custodians; leave them out unless a community is involved and agrees.

**Other fire stories**, if more are wanted: in the Rigveda, Mātariśvan brings fire (Agni) down to people; many Algonquian peoples tell of a trickster hero stealing fire. Check each for a "cost" before using it, since the cost is the point of the sequence.

**Panel 8** carries the shared blame from the bible's first law. If any line is cut, keep "There are no villains in this story."
