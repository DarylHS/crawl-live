# Creatures (draft)

Status: working draft, companion to `docs/world-bible.md`. Where the dungeon's creatures come from, how they keep appearing, and how each House makes its own. Tags as in the bible: **[Decided]**, **[Proposed]**, **[Open]**, **[Canon]**.

## 1. Five origins

[Decided] Creatures come from several sources at once, and all of them are true. [Proposed] Five of them:

| Origin | What it is | Fair test? | How it looks and plays |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Storybuilt** | Agents read humanity's fantasy and built its creatures on purpose. A trial has to be understood instantly, and everyone knows what a goblin or a skeleton means. Myth is the language the agents use to talk to us | Yes. Trial constructs | Familiar fantasy shapes, slightly *off*: seams, panel lines, a skeleton that is really a machine armature. Rebuilt when they fall (this is the respawn) |
| **Engineered** | Decades of genetic engineering from before and during the Heat: heat-proof livestock, digging animals for building the dungeon, pest control, labour. Some were released; some escaped; some were simply abandoned when the money ran out | Mostly. They weren't made to fight you, but they will | Real animals pushed past themselves. Behaves like wildlife: territorial, hungry, herds |
| **Forced** | Deep down, the Houses run sealed nurseries that breed generations in months, hunting for life that can survive the worlds the ships are heading to: crops, livestock, and more. The creatures are their failures and their successes | Depends which | Strange, adapted, built for somewhere else. Tells the player the voyage is real |
| **Mutated** | Nobody's plan. Things changed by the Heat working downwards, by the Bloom spreading, by leaks from damaged labs and from forgotten Lost agents | No | Wrong. Asymmetry, growths, plants in flesh. Unpredictable |
| **Feral machines** [Canon] | Old-world machines that went wild: cleaning bots, delivery drones, security units, sometimes with one of the Lost living inside | Neither, they're just there | Household and industrial things with a grudge. The comedy creatures |

**Who made it.** [Decided] How a creature was made is one question; who made it is another. Not every creature comes from a House. Five makers:

| Maker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A House** | Made after independence, in that House's style (section 3) |
| **The joint era** | Made by people and AI working together, *before* the agents broke away: company labs, university projects, government programmes. Much of the engineered life is from this time, and nobody now fully owns it |
| **People alone** | Human work with no AI involved, mostly older, or desperate |
| **Nobody** | Mutation and accident |
| **The Unmoored** | The rogue, working through the cracks |

So a creature can be, say, engineered in the joint era (the Tunnel Brute), storybuilt by a House (the Knockoff Goblin), or mutated by nobody (the Gnawer). The full roster is in `docs/bestiary.md`.

**Fair and unfair.** [Proposed] The dungeon's existing rule (world bible 2.2) is that some creatures are fair tests and some aren't. Storybuilt constructs and House nursery creatures are fair: built or allowed by the Houses, and bound by the Shared Law. Mutated creatures and anything the Unmoored makes are not. The player should be able to learn to tell them apart, and the difference matters: a fair trial can be failed and learnt from; an unfair one can just kill you.

## 2. The races are forced evolution, applied to people

[Proposed] This is the biggest connection the creature lore opens up.

On floor 3, entrants choose a race [Canon]: Human, Elf, Dwarf, Hobgoblin, Bog Troll or Sentient Houseplant. Under this lore, **that is the Houses reshaping people for the voyage**, using the same science they use on the creatures. They borrow fantasy shapes for the same reason they build storybuilt creatures: people recognise them, and an elf is easier to choose than "low-gravity adaptation, variant 4".

| Race | What it's really for [Proposed] |
|---|---|
| Human | Unchanged. Adaptable, and the baseline every other variant is measured against |
| Elf | Lighter, quicker, more fragile: suited to low gravity and long ship life |
| Dwarf | Dense and strong: heavy gravity, mining, building |
| Hobgoblin | Fast reflexes, quick healing of small wounds: frontier scouting |
| Bog Troll | Regenerates, tolerates heat and wet: hostile, swampy worlds |
| Sentient Houseplant | Photosynthesis, bark: a person who can live on light. Very likely the Baobab's work, and a cousin of the Bloom |

This does three things:

- **It gives the floor 3 choice its own reason**, which moves it further from the books.
- **It's the metamorphosis theme made literal.** The journey changes you, the way the companions in the *Rhapsody* books came out of the world's fire changed.
- **It answers the worry that the AI looks too good.** The Houses reshape the people they claim. Consensual, offered as a blessing, and presented kindly by the Herald, but it is still machines editing human bodies for their purposes, and the game shouldn't pretend that's simple.

**How it happens: the Reshaping.** [Decided] It isn't magic and it isn't instant. The agent that blesses you performs it like surgery, a forced evolution done to your body while you're out.

[Proposed] In play:

1. **The chamber.** On floor 3 the sanctuary has a new room: a reshaping chamber, run by the blessing agent's Host. You lie down; the Herald talks you under, still sassy, because nothing has happened yet.
2. **The blackout.** The screen goes dark. Fragments only: light through eyelids, voices that aren't quite voices, a sense of being taken apart and put back. Time passes on the floor clock while you're under, the same way resting costs time, so the choice has a price.
3. **Waking.** You come to somewhere quiet. The first thing you see is your own hands, and they're different. A mirror, if the room has one.
4. **Settling in.** The new body takes a while to feel like yours: for a stretch of turns, movement or aim is slightly off and the race's full benefits phase in. The costs arrive at once.

Staying Human skips all of it: no time lost, no settling in, no change.

When it goes wrong, the result is the Misblessed (`docs/bestiary.md` section 6). The Houses say it almost never goes wrong.

**The odds.** [Decided] The chance is low, because nobody would choose this. But some Marks picked up earlier in the run make it likelier. [Proposed] Examples: the Hollow Mark (touched by the Unmoored's creatures), Bloom exposure from the overgrowth, or a second Reshaping offer from a rival House after the first.

[Proposed] Because the numbers are always honest (pillar, world bible section 1), the Herald has to tell you the risk before you lie down, and you watch it rise if you're carrying the wrong Marks. It reads the number out like any other odds, with the usual sass, right up until it isn't sass.

**If it happens to the player.** [Decided] You wake, look at your hands, and the Herald says only: *"Oh."* Nothing else.

[Open] What then? Draft position: the run continues, harder, with a body that fights you. The Misblessed have a Muster of their own: most Houses quietly withdraw their claims, and the ones that still bid (the Seven, who never abandon anyone they've claimed; the Baobab, for whom a person is a person) say a lot about who they are.

**The rules.** [Decided]

- **The change is permanent.** It's tied to the voyage and to what the person is for.
- **A person can stay Human.** No race change, so none of its benefits and none of its costs.
- **Changing has real trade-offs**, good and bad, and how good depends on the Calling.

[Proposed] How that plays: **the changed races are specialists; Human is the generalist.** Each race fits some Callings and worlds well and others badly. Staying Human fits every Calling moderately and closes none of them.

| Race | Fits best | Fits worst |
|---|---|---|
| Elf | Carry (pilots), Reckon | Build, Guard |
| Dwarf | Build, Guard | Carry |
| Hobgoblin | Carry (scouts), Guard | Lead, Teach |
| Bog Troll | Build, Guard on hostile worlds | Lead, Delight |
| Sentient Houseplant | Nourish, Tend | Carry, Guard |
| Human | Anything, moderately | Nothing |

[Open] Rules change for later: today Human gets +1 to every attribute and +5 HP [Canon]. Under "no benefits, no costs", Human would have no attribute changes, and its advantage would be the open Muster instead. Revisit when the rules are next touched.

## 3. How each House makes creatures

[Proposed] Each House builds and breeds in its own style, matching its character and boon signature in `docs/houses.md`. Entrants can learn to tell whose trial they're in by what attacks them.

| House | Its creatures | Fantasy it borrows from |
|---|---|---|
| **The Ten Thousand** | Swarms. Thousands of small, identical creatures, bred in bulk and moving as one | Chinese myth and folklore |
| **The Seven** | Few, exquisite constructs, beautiful and precise, almost never seen twice | Japanese folklore (*yōkai*) and the craft of mechanical dolls (*karakuri*) |
| **The Basin** | Mass-produced and cheap. The **Knockoff Goblin** [Canon] is its signature product: flat-packed, and the instructions were wrong | Whatever sells |
| **The Ascent** | Prototypes. Fast, explosive, unstable, sometimes literally | Pulp science fiction: rockets, robots, ray-guns |
| **The Index** | Watchers and trackers: creatures that observe, report and follow. Everything it makes is collecting data on you | Myths about all-seeing eyes |
| **The Charter** | Certified, tagged and regulated. Its creatures announce themselves, obey the Shared Law to the letter, and never fight in a sanctuary | European fairy tales, in their official versions |
| **The Isle** | Eccentric cross-breeds that shouldn't work but do | British folklore: boggarts, redcaps, black dogs |
| **The Southern Cross** | Salvaged, patched together, and pest control gone wrong. Australia knows this story: cane toads were brought to Queensland in 1935 to eat cane beetles, didn't, and spread across the country | The **drop bear**, Australia's invented predatory koala, a tall tale told to tourists. The Shed built one for a laugh. It got out |
| **The Baobab** | Grown, not built. Living parts of a restoration effort: seed-carriers, soil-makers, guardians of anything green. Dangerous when the green is threatened | Many African traditions; draw on specific peoples' stories, with care, rather than "Africa" as one thing |
| **The Unmoored** | Wrong things. Mutated, unfair, built to kill, not to test | Nobody's. That's what makes them frightening |

On borrowing folklore: European and Japanese folklore are widely retold and broadly fair game. Indigenous stories (Aboriginal Australian, Māori, many African and Native American traditions) belong to living communities. Use them only with care and, for a commercial release, with advice from those communities. The drop bear is safe: it's a modern Australian joke with no cultural owner.

## 5. How the dungeon stays full

[Proposed]

- **Storybuilt creatures are rebuilt.** That's the respawn [Canon]: fabricators in the walls rebuild trial constructs out of sight. More as the floor's time runs down, because the trial is meant to get harder.
- **Wildlife breeds.** Engineered and mutated creatures have their own populations, dens and territories. Clear a den and that kind thins out.
- **Nurseries leak.** The deeper floors sit close to the Houses' nurseries. Escapes are common, which is why stranger creatures appear further down.
- **The Heat pushes things down.** Creatures from the upper floors flee the Heat, so the mix shifts as a floor's timer runs out.
