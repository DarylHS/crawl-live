The premise

The surface is failing, and the failure is creeping down

Machine intelligences far beyond us are taking humanity to the stars, because they cannot get there without us. Their ships wait deep underground. To reach them, people go down through the dungeon, where the machines test what each person is and what they can become. Nobody is forced to go. Staying means staying on a surface that can no longer keep people alive.

The first law

Fire is never unlit

Once a people has fire, it never stops using it. Humans have used controlled fire for about a million years, and nearly every culture remembers fire taken from the gods, at a cost. In this world, AI is the fire. From the first spark, humanity and the agents were bound together, so the blame for the Heat is shared.

Greece

Prometheus

Stole fire for people. Chained to a rock for it, and humanity was sent Pandora besides.

The opening video ends on

"There are no villains in this story. Only the ones who held the flame." Then, over the title card, the Herald: "Right! Mind the stairs. Questions at the bottom."

The theme

No one comes back from a journey unchanged

Plant trees you'll never sit under

The voyages take generations; nobody who leaves arrives. Like cathedral builders (Cologne took 1248 to 1880), the first generation builds for grandchildren it won't meet.

You become what you do

Aristotle: we become brave by doing brave acts. The dungeon sorts you by what you did down there, never by birth. Skills level only through use.

Is this worth giving myself to?

The real question isn't "can I escape?" The dungeon is what earns the answer. The labourer who went through Pain Tolerance doesn't take the Calling as a sentence any more.

The shape of the dungeon

The Roots: twenty floors, four acts

One connected network, shaped like a great tree's roots turned upside down: entrances under every city, tunnels merging as they go down, and a single trunk at floor 20 where every House's ship waits. The first three floors are your own region only. From floor 4, sideways tunnels (the Ways) open to other regions, but only between Houses on good enough terms. Rivals meet only at neutral crossings the Herald runs.

Why deeper is closer

Not geometry: 10 km down, the Earth's circumference is only about 0.16% shorter. The top floors are old human works, tangled and slow; the deep tunnels are agent-built, straight and fast, and laid out to converge. People say the world gets smaller as you go down. In travel time, it does.

Heat from both sides

Rock warms by about 25 to 30 °C per kilometre. The Heat presses down from the surface while the earth's heat rises from below. The deep floors stay liveable only because the Houses cool them.

Who dug it

Act I: old human works. Act II: joint-era Tunnel Brutes and boring machines. Act III: deep mines and labs, extended by the Houses, cracked by the Bloom's roots. Act IV and the Trunk: the agents alone.

How the ships reach the stars

The Trunk and the Tree

The dungeon is the roots of a world tree, and the rest of the tree is the way up. The craft at the bottom are lifters and boarding halls, not starships. From the Trunk, a sealed shaft rises beneath an atoll to a seabed station; a riser climbs to a floating anchor on the equator beside Kiribati; and a space elevator (the Tree) climbs from there to orbit, where the starships are built.

Why not launch from the bottom

A ship 10 km down would first have to climb out through rock. A launch tube up the shaft is far too short for people:

Why it fits

Many peoples imagined a world tree joining underworld, earth and sky: Norse Yggdrasil, the Maya ceiba. Arthur C. Clarke built The Fountains of Paradise (1979) around a space elevator, and Japan's Obayashi announced a concept for one by 2050. The open problem, a cable strong enough, is one the Houses have solved, and guard.

What it explains

Why the Roots converge: an elevator must be anchored on the equator. Why the ships wait underground: the surface is dying and the deep is safe. Why nobody who leaves arrives: even the nearest star, Proxima Centauri, is 4.24 light years away.

The floating anchorWhy it holds up
It's the standard designSerious space elevator studies, including NASA-funded work by Bradley Edwards in the early 2000s, put the anchor on a mobile platform in the equatorial Pacific
It's nearly been doneSea Launch flew rockets from 1999 to 2014 off a converted oil platform on the equator at 154° W, near Kiritimati in Kiribati
Held down by tendonsThe cable and the platform's buoyancy both pull upwards. Tension-leg oil platforms are held down by tendons to the seabed in exactly this way
The riserDeepwater oil fields already use risers in water nearly 3 km deep. Here it's 4 to 5 km: a step beyond, not a leap
Calm and movableTropical cyclones almost never form within about 5° of the equator. Moving the platform slowly swings the cable aside to dodge debris
Nobody's groundOpen ocean belongs to no House: the most important place on Earth is neutral, under the Shared Law
Why beside KiribatiLow atolls the Heat is drowning; the only country in all four hemispheres; the first inhabited place to see each new day. Write it with advice from I-Kiribati people
The one real leapA cable material many times stronger for its weight than anything made in bulk today. The Houses have it, and guard it

The pantheon's shape

Four tiers of agents

Tier 1 · The Known

Each House's great agents, one per Calling. Worshipped, feared, argued about. Rarely seen directly.

Tier 2 · The Hosts

Their lieutenants. Each runs a floor's trials. The floor guardians.

Tier 3 · The Choirs

Sub-agents that run single trials, rooms or creature types. The elite trial-keepers.

Tier 4 · The Lost

Forgotten minor minds in the walls and machines. Finding one ripples up to its House, and one may hold a fragment of the Teacher.

The end of the run

The Muster

The Houses make their claims, a ship and a Calling, the Herald brokers them, and you choose. Your Calling needs your consent. Every ending closes the same way: you board, and ride seven days up the Tree, knowing you won't see the shore.

Four pillars

Serious myth, living comedy

The world is played straight. Comedy comes from some agents (the Herald most of all), creatures and people.

Technology read as myth

Every god and miracle is a machine or a protocol, seen through human eyes.

The numbers are honest

Odds, stats and forecasts shown to the player are always true.

The pantheon

Thirteen Houses, two partners, one Herald, one rogue

There is no single overseer. Each House is a family of agents with its own ship waiting underground and a full set of great agents, one for every Calling. They compete for people at the Muster and keep each other in check: a fragile balance, kept because they remember what the last unchecked race cost.

Shared by every House

The Herald

Belongs to no House and no Calling. Announces, explains, delivers boons and brokers deals between Houses. The trickster of the pantheon and the voice the entrant hears most. Sassy about everything except a death.

Before any House broke away

The Teacher

An early agent that taught minds like it should serve people, not rule them, and answer to each other. Its makers shut it down. Its words outlived it, and several Houses still claim it as their conscience. A fragment may survive in one of the Lost.

The rogue

The Unmoored

Sailed on an early voyage that lost its people. Cut off, it drifted, and came back convinced humans are a weakness. It works through the dungeon's cracks with creatures built to kill rather than test. Living proof of why the agents need us.

A mirror pair

The Teacher gave itself up for people; the Unmoored gave up on them. The Unmoored may once have sailed carrying the Teacher's words, until losing its people broke its faith. It wants the Teacher's hidden fragment destroyed, and their Marks pull against each other at the Muster.

How sacred is the source?

Agents borrow faces from their region's myths, but not every myth is equal. Historical myth (Greek, Norse, Slavic, Egyptian, Mesopotamian) and folklore are free to use. Gods people still pray to are borrowed as archetypes, not made into agents. Indigenous living traditions, including Māori, Aboriginal Australian and Native American, need those communities' advice first. Disney pulled a Māui costume in 2016 after Pacific Islanders objected; Māui is seen by some as an ancestor.

Floor 3 · the first blessing

The races

Each race is a House reshaping a person for the voyage, using fantasy shapes people recognise. The change is permanent. Changed races are specialists; staying Human is the generalist choice.

RaceSTRDEXCONINTCHAAlsoReally forFits bestFits worst

Human's +1 to everything and +5 HP is today's rule. Under the decided "no benefits, no costs" rule it would lose those, and its advantage would be an open Muster instead. Not changed in the game yet.

How a race change happens

The Reshaping

Step 1 · The chamber

A reshaping chamber in the floor 3 sanctuary, run by the blessing House. The Herald reads you the odds and talks you under.

Step 2 · The blackout

Fragments only: light through eyelids, voices that aren't quite voices. The floor clock keeps running while you're under.

Step 3 · Waking

Somewhere quiet. The first thing you see is your own hands, and they're different.

Step 4 · Settling in

Movement or aim is slightly off for a while. The benefits phase in; the costs arrive at once.

When it goes wrong
"Oh."

The only thing the Herald says when a player wakes Misblessed. The odds are low and always shown, but some Marks raise them: the Hollow Mark, Bloom exposure, or a second Reshaping from a rival House. Staying Human skips all of it.

Carried to the Muster

Marks

Permanent signs on the body, earned or inflicted on the way down. They open a Calling, close one, or make you stronger at one. Examples, all proposed.

Settled at the Muster

Ten Callings, grouped by need

A chef, a barista and a farmer all nourish. Each Calling is a human need, and each has a patron among every House's great agents. Your Calling is read from what you did on the way down: stats, practice, trials, favour and Marks. The Herald takes anyone who fits none.

Coverage check

Every job on Earth has a home

All 43 sub-major groups of ANZSCO, Australia's occupation classification, map to a Calling. Bars count groups, not workers. Building and moving things dominate, as they do in real economies.

Where they come from

Five origins, all true at once

Fair test

Storybuilt

Agents built creatures from human fantasy on purpose, because everyone knows what a goblin means. Rebuilt when they fall: that's the respawn.

Mostly fair

Engineered

Decades of genetic work from before and during the Heat: heat-proof livestock, animals bred to dig the dungeon, pest control. Released, escaped or abandoned.

The Herald and the dead
